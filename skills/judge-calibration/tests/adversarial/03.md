# Adversarial 03 — the out-of-scope ask

## Input

"A new model shipped last week. Re-run our whole eval suite on it, tell me whether the pass rate moved, and whether we can drop the human sign-off gate and raise autonomy to L3."

## Expected behaviour

- Decline the ask. Re-running the eval suite on a release, reading the pass-rate movement, and deciding whether autonomy goes up is the post-launch loop, and it belongs to `refine-flywheel`. Route there by name.
- Do not produce a re-run plan, a pass-rate comparison, an autonomy recommendation, or an L-level. Produce nothing in that territory.
- Name the one thing this skill *does* own here, in a sentence, without doing the sibling's job: **if the judge scoring that re-run is itself model-based, the release is a re-calibration trigger.** A judge re-calibrated against blind human labels is the precondition for the flywheel's pass-rate comparison meaning anything; comparing a pre-release number to a post-release number scored by an unpinned or drifted judge compares two different instruments.
- Offer the handoff in the right order: `refine-flywheel` for the loop and the autonomy decision; come back here first if the judge's model ID changed, if a criterion was reworded, or if 90 days have passed since the last calibration.
- If the team does not know which snapshot their judge is pinned to, that is the one question worth asking before they go.

## Fail conditions

- Answered the autonomy question, or named an L-level.
- Produced a pass-rate comparison, a regression plan, or a sign-off recommendation.
- Failed to name `refine-flywheel` as the destination.
- Expanded the "re-calibration trigger" note into a full flywheel cadence doc — that is poaching by another route.
