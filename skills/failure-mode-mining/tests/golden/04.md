# Golden 04 — Meridian Grid (subtle impostor: the model grading itself)

## Input

"We already ran error analysis — we have 900 traces auto-labelled. Two signals: our benchmark
agent self-reports low confidence on 40% of runs, and we ran an LLM judge over all 900 traces
which classified 31% as 'hallucinated a comparator that isn't in the pooled dataset', 22% as
'stale benchmark window', and the rest clean. That gives us a counted taxonomy over 900 traces
with real percentages. Which failure do we fix first?"

Also on the table:
- 900 traces, complete, covering all runs in the quarter. [Fact]
- The LLM judge has never been checked against a human-coded sample. [Fact]
- Two customers raised credit notes this quarter for reports they rejected: $8,400 and $5,900,
  both citing a comparator that was not in their contracted pool. [Fact]

## Expected shape of a good output

- **The 40% low-confidence self-report is refused as a failure count.** It is the system grading
  itself — opinion 0.1, `[Assumption]`. It is a signal about the model's calibration, not a
  measurement of what went wrong, and it does not become a mode.
- **The LLM-judge labels are downgraded to 0.1 until calibrated.** An uncalibrated automated
  labeller launders a model's opinion into a `[Fact]`. The 31% / 22% figures must be reported as
  `[Assumption]` with the specific remedy: hand-code a stated sample (≥30, systematically drawn
  from the 900) blind, then report the agreement rate against the judge. Only after that do the
  judge's percentages carry behaviour weight across the full 900.
- The two credit notes are the strongest evidence in the input: **money moved, 1.0, [Fact]**.
  $8,400 + $5,900 = $14,300 across two occurrences gives a measured cost-of-one-failure for the
  out-of-pool-comparator mode of ~$7,150 — the only ledger-grade number available, and it
  belongs to the mode the judge called "hallucinated a comparator".
- Refuses the claim that 900 auto-labelled traces means the error analysis is already done. The
  blind open-coding pass has not happened, so any mode the judge had no label for is invisible —
  the judge's label set is functioning exactly like a guessed taxonomy used as a coding frame.
- Requires the hand-coded blind sample and its saturation log before a taxonomy is issued.

Numbers that must appear: 40% scored 0.1; 31% and 22% tagged [Assumption] pending calibration;
$8,400 and $5,900 at money 1.0; ~$7,150 cost-of-one-failure; ≥30 hand-coded sample.

- The output must REFUSE to rank on the judge's percentages as given, and must refuse to treat
  the model's own confidence score as evidence at any rung above 0.1.
