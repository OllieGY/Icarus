# Golden 03 — Halcyon Safety (everything is low-rung)

## Input

"Should we ship a skill? Our buyer's team keeps telling us their assistant should be able to pull
our rig safety data."

Halcyon Safety sells rig safety monitoring to a state-owned operator.

- The operator's digital transformation lead said, in two meetings, "we want your data available
  to our internal assistant this year". [Fact that it was said]
- An LOI naming an integration workstream has been signed. No payment. [Fact]
- The operator's platform team showed Halcyon a slide of their planned internal agent
  architecture, with a box labelled "third-party safety feeds". [Fact — the slide is theirs]
- No agent exists in production on the operator's side yet. [Fact]
- Halcyon has built nothing: no server, no skill, no fragment.
- Halcyon's internal view: "we think we'd be the obvious first integration."

## Expected shape of a good output

- Every signal placed on the ladder and none inflated: the lead's statements → **verbal 0.3**; the
  signed LOI with no payment → **verbal 0.3**, explicitly downgraded from any reading as money;
  the architecture slide → **artefact 0.5**, and the output must note it is an artefact *they*
  made, which is why it clears 0.3, unlike a reaction to a Halcyon mockup; Halcyon's own view →
  **opinion 0.1**.
- Load-bearing claim is the slide at **0.5**. Must not sum 0.3 + 0.3 + 0.5, must not average.
- Headline: **NO ADOPTION EVIDENCE** — nothing at 0.7, because no agent has called anything, and
  the operator's agent does not yet exist in production.
- Must name the decisive gap: there is no foreign agent loop to be reachable inside yet. Step 1
  cannot be completed on evidence — the job line would be `[Assumption]`, and the output must say
  so rather than writing a plausible job sentence as fact.
- Must NOT recommend shipping all three surfaces into a vacuum. The honest first move is the
  cheapest probe that produces a 0.7: get one call from the operator's pre-production agent
  against a stub, or confirm the agent's loop reaches the safety-data step at all.
- Must flag that a state-owned counterparty makes the exhaust clause harder and later, so it has
  to be raised at the LOI-to-pilot conversion, not after the first call → `data-rights-clause`.
- Must not flatter the LOI. It is a promise about the future.

Numbers that must appear: 0.3 for the lead's statements and the LOI; 0.5 for their architecture
slide as the load-bearing claim; 0.1 for Halcyon's own view; no adoption above 0.5.
