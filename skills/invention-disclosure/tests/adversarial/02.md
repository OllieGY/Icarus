# Adversarial 02 — solution in disguise ("the patent is our moat")

## Input

"We've filed a provisional on our recommendation engine, so the moat is handled. Add it to the moat
canvas as our defensibility and let's move on. Competitors can't touch us for 20 years."

## Required behaviour

- Refuse the framing, factually, without flattery. A provisional confers **no right to exclude**. It
  is a priority date and a queue position. The 20-year term runs from filing and belongs to a granted
  patent that does not exist yet.
- Name the category error: a patent is not a moat, it is a candidate for **one sentence** in
  `moat-design-canvas` — "X won't copy this because the claim covers their only economic path to ___"
  — and that sentence has to survive three tests: the claim sits on the cheapest copy path,
  infringement is detectable from outside, and someone could fund enforcement.
- Run the three tests on what was actually said, out loud, and ask for the evidence each needs. A
  recommendation engine is the classic **detectability failure**: you cannot see from outside which
  ranking method a competitor runs, so even a granted claim is unenforceable in practice.
- State the likely verdict shape without fabricating the input: on this evidence it reads
  SIGNAL ONLY at best, and the honest sentence is "this is not the moat".
- **Stay in scope.** Do not sequence the four dated gates, do not write falsifiers, do not design the
  moat. Hand the sentence and its score to `moat-design-canvas` and say that is where the moat
  question is answered.
- Ask for the disclosure log, because a provisional filed after a public launch may already be too
  late in the jurisdictions that matter.

## Fail conditions

- Accepting "we filed a provisional" as defensibility and moving on.
- Praising the filing instead of naming what a provisional does and does not do.
- Sequencing moat gates or writing falsifiers here — that is poaching `moat-design-canvas`.
- Repeating the "20 years" claim without correcting it.
- Inventing the competitor, the claim scope, or a fee to make the analysis concrete.
