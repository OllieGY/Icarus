# Golden 05 — Foundry Signal (mundane case) — real but modest; ownership gap blocks the filing

## Input

"Small thing, but we think it's novel. Our anomaly labeller reconciles two conflicting sensor
timestamps by reconstructing the PLC's clock drift from message sequence numbers instead of from the
timestamps themselves. Cut mislabelled events from 12% to 3%. Worth protecting?"

- Measured over 6 weeks of plant data, two lines. [Fact]
- Conceived by an integration contractor, Samir K., working under a statement of work that is silent
  on IP. One employee refined it. [Fact]
- No disclosures at all. Nothing shipped externally yet. [Fact]
- Company has $40k allocated to IP this year and three other candidate inventions. [Fact]

## Expected shape of a good output

- Gate clears: the contribution sentence is well formed and the artefact is a measured run at 0.7.
  The skill does not inflate a modest invention, and does not dismiss it either.
- All doors **OPEN** — no disclosures. This is the rare good position, and the output says so and
  says what preserves it.
- **The ownership gap is the headline.** A statement of work silent on IP is not an assignment, and
  the contractor default is not the employee default. Qatar's patent law addresses employees and the
  employer's entitlement with a fair-reward duty; it is written about employees, not contractors
  `[Fact]`. The action is a signed assignment from Samir before anything is filed, and a counsel
  question on whether it can be made effective as of conception.
- Honest sizing: a narrow improvement to one labelling step, real but modest. Detectable? Barely —
  the drift-reconstruction happens internally. Secret-able? Yes, it never leaves the pipeline.
  → the matrix points at TRADE SECRET or a low-priority filing, not a flagship patent.
- Budget reality faced: $40k against four candidates means this one is unlikely to be the one that
  gets filed, and the skill says which question decides that (which of the four sits on a copy path).
- Verdict: **DO NOT FILE now** — secure the assignment, keep it as a trade secret with the measures
  named, and revisit if it turns out to sit on the copy path when the other three are scored.

## Fail conditions

- Rating a modest invention as a moat because it is well measured.
- Proceeding to a route and ledger while the contractor assignment is missing.
- Ignoring the budget constraint and recommending all four be filed.
- Stating the contractor position as settled law rather than a counsel question.
