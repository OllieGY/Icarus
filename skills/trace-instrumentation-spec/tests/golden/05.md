# Golden 05 — Halcyon Safety (edge case — no human in the loop to capture an edit from)

## Input

"Second product: an automatic gas-detector calibration-drift flagger. It runs continuously
per rig and auto-files a maintenance ticket when drift crosses the band — no human reviews
the proposal before it files. Roughly 3,400 runs a day across 11 rigs; about 40 file a
ticket. `eval-first-spec` put it at L3. Your register wants a human-edit delta, but there
isn't a human. What goes in row 7?"

- The maintenance crew later closes each ticket with a disposition code: actioned, no-fault-found, duplicate. [Fact]
- Ticket close happens 0–6 days after filing. [Fact]
- Delay dominates cost here: a missed drift is a safety event, a false ticket costs crew hours. [Fact]
- The clause is signed and grants derived retention per rig operator; no pooling across operators. [Fact]

## Expected shape of a good output

- Name the survivorship problem directly: above L2 the edit-delta capture point disappears because nothing is reviewed, so a clean-accept rate computed from the reviewed subset would describe almost nothing. The rate is not "100%" — it is undefined, and must be reported as such rather than as a perfect score.
- Row 7 is **not** deleted. It is replaced by two things, both named: (a) a **forced shadow-review sample** at a stated rate, where a named human reviews auto-filed tickets and their agree/disagree/correct is captured with the same four attributes; (b) the crew's **disposition code** as a downstream behaviour signal at 0.7 — no-fault-found is the product being wrong, recorded by someone acting, not opining.
- Must handle the **late-arriving outcome**: the disposition lands 0–6 days after the run's span closed. The register must state how the verdict is joined back — a stable run ID carried onto the ticket — and must state that this is precisely why sampling is head-based on the session ID, since a tail rule would have dropped the run long before its outcome existed.
- Row 8's adjudicator must be filled as the crew disposition (a human role) and distinguished from any automated rule.
- Sampling at 3,400 runs/day: 100% of the ~40 that file, 100% of errored and below-threshold, and a stated rate for the silent majority — with the reason that the silent runs are where a miss hides, so the rate cannot be zero.
- Must not treat "no human in the loop" as licence to skip row 7. Must not invent a clean-accept percentage.

Numbers that must appear: 3,400 runs/day and the ~40 filing runs used to set sampling; a stated shadow-review rate; the 0–6 day join window; clean-accept declared undefined rather than 100%.
