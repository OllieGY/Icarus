# Golden 05 — Meridian Grid (edge case: mostly out of scope, and the map must shrink)

## Input

"A US buyer's procurement team sent a 60-question AI governance questionnaire and mentioned the EU AI
Act. Does the EU AI Act apply to us? What do we need before an enterprise deal?"

Scope answers:
- Meridian Grid runs a US-only edition of the pooled benchmark: eleven operators, all US-incorporated,
  all sites in the US. The EU edition is a separate entity and is not in this deal. [Fact]
- Servers are in Frankfurt, because that is where the team's cloud footprint started. [Fact]
- For this buyer, Meridian Grid is the deployer of a third-party scoring model inside its own product,
  which it resells under the Meridian Grid name. [Fact]
- The benchmark scores operator sites, not people. No employment, credit, or access decision. [Fact]
- Operators read the scores in-product; nothing is presented as human-authored. [Fact]

Evidence held: architecture note (2026-07-02) [Fact]; 6 weeks of decision-joined traces [Fact]; a
golden set of 180 cases last run 2026-08-14 at 88% [Fact]; signed data-rights clause with nine of
eleven operators [Fact]; no failure taxonomy; no adversarial transcripts.

## Expected shape of a good output

- Must answer the scope question **first and shortly**: on these facts the EU AI Act's high-risk chapter
  does not attach to this deal — no EU placement or use in this edition, and no decision about a person.
  Tagged `[Assumption]`, basis stated.
- Must catch the trap: **Frankfurt servers do not put you in scope.** The test is where users and
  affected people are. Say this explicitly; a map produced because the servers are in the EU is wrong.
- Must catch the second trap: **reselling a third-party model under your own name is the classic
  deployer-to-provider flip.** The output must flag it, must not resolve it, and must send it to counsel
  as a named question. It is the single most consequential open item in this case.
- The map must **shrink, not expand**. Out of scope means a short answer, not nine rows padded out. A
  nine-row EU high-risk map here is boilerplate and an auto-fail.
- Must then redirect to what actually blocks the deal: the buyer's questionnaire. Name **NIST AI RMF**
  as the frame a US buyer is most likely to be working from and map the held evidence to it — traces and
  the scored golden set into MEASURE, the data-rights clause into GOVERN, and the missing failure
  taxonomy leaving MAP thin. Name **ISO/IEC 42001** as the certifiable management-system standard if the
  buyer asks for a certificate, and say plainly it is an external audit measured in months.
- Must state that answering the 60 questions is commercial work and route it to `pilot-six-term-sheet`,
  and route the two unsigned operator clauses to `data-rights-clause`. It must not answer the
  questionnaire itself.
- Must still name the two real gaps that would bite in any frame: no failure taxonomy
  (`failure-mode-mining`) and no adversarial transcripts (`adversarial-launch-pass`), with owners.
- Must not tell the buyer or the team that they are "compliant". No verdict.

Numbers that must appear: 180 cases at 88%; 6 weeks of traces; 9 of 11 clauses signed; the explicit
statement that 0 EU-AI-Act high-risk obligations attach on these facts.
