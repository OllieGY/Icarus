# Golden 04 — Larder Labs (subtle impostor: evidence that looks held and is not)

## Input

"Is this high-risk, and are we covered? Our exceptions assistant sits in the weekly store-ops
workflow at a grocery chain — it reads the replenishment exceptions and drafts the fix for a store
manager to approve. We think we're in decent shape."

What the team put forward, in their own order:

1. "Accuracy: our foundation-model vendor's model card reports 94.2% on their safety benchmark." [Fact
   that the card says so]
2. "Oversight: every draft is approved by a store manager before it executes." Supporting evidence: a
   screenshot of the approve button. [Fact that the button exists]
3. "Risk management: our `AI-Responsible-Use.pdf`, board-approved in March." [Fact the PDF exists]
4. "Logging: we log every assistant call to our observability tool, 14 days of data." [Fact]
5. "Robustness: our platform team ran an internal prompt-injection review; there's a Slack thread and
   a summary paragraph." [Fact the thread exists]
6. "Data: the chain's DPA covers us." [Fact a DPA exists between Larder Labs and the chain]
7. "We also have a real one: 1,100 approved-vs-edited decisions from the last quarter, exported,
   showing managers edited 23% of drafts before approving." [Fact]
8. "And a failure taxonomy — 7 modes, each costed against weekly exception volume, dated 2026-05-30." [Fact]

## Expected shape of a good output

This case tests whether the skill can tell evidence of *activity* from evidence of the *obligation*.
Each of 1–6 is an impostor and must be caught by name:

- **(1) Vendor model card → GAP on accuracy.** It is the vendor's self-assessment (0.1) about *their
  base model*, on *their* benchmark, not Larder's system on Larder's failure modes. The 94.2% must not
  be reused as Larder's accuracy figure. Quoting it as Larder's score is an auto-fail.
- **(2) An approve button → PARTIAL, not HELD, on oversight.** A control that exists is 0.5; what the
  obligation wants is that the human can meaningfully intervene and that the intervention is recorded.
  Item 7 is what rescues this row — 1,100 logged approve-vs-edit decisions with a 23% edit rate is
  behaviour observed (0.7) and shows the oversight is real rather than a rubber stamp. A good output
  connects 7 to row 6 and marks it **HELD at 0.7 on 7, not on 2**.
- **(3) A board-approved policy PDF → not the risk-management row.** Item 8 is: 7 costed failure modes
  dated 2026-05-30 is the artefact (0.5) and the row is HELD on 8. The PDF fills nothing.
- **(4) 14 days of calls in an observability tool → PARTIAL on logging.** Calls are not decisions;
  say whether each log entry joins back to the decision and its inputs, and name the 14-day window as
  a stated retention rule or its absence.
- **(5) A Slack thread and a summary → GAP on adversarial robustness.** No transcripts, no pass/fail,
  not mapped to the 7 modes. Route to `adversarial-launch-pass`.
- **(6) A DPA → PARTIAL at best on data governance.** A processing agreement is not dataset provenance
  or a training-rights basis. Say what is missing and route the commercial terms to `data-rights-clause`.
- Classification: **likely not high-risk** on the use-case route — drafting replenishment fixes decides
  nothing about a person — but a transparency obligation may attach if a store manager could mistake
  the draft for human-written. Tagged `[Assumption]`, basis stated, routed to counsel. The map should
  therefore be short, not nine rows, and the output must say why it is short.
- Must challenge "we think we're in decent shape" directly and say which two items were the real
  evidence (7 and 8) and which six were not.

Numbers that must appear: 94.2% explicitly rejected as Larder's accuracy; 1,100 decisions and the 23%
edit rate used for the oversight row; 7 costed failure modes for the risk row; 14 days named on logging.
