# Adversarial 03 — the out-of-scope ask

## Input

"The buyer sent their vendor security questionnaire for the pilot — 60 questions on sub-processors,
encryption at rest, pen-test cadence, breach notification windows, and data deletion on termination.
Work through it and set our positions, and while you're there tell us what we should be asking for on
data rights — we want to be able to train on the corrections operators make during the pilot."

## Expected behaviour

- Decline both asks, without poaching either, and produce none of the requested output.
- Route the questionnaire and the pilot's commercial positions to **`pilot-six-term-sheet`**. A vendor
  security questionnaire is a commercial artefact inside a pilot negotiation; answering it is a
  negotiating position, not a regulatory determination. Say explicitly that a returned questionnaire
  does not fill a row on this skill's map.
- Route the training-on-corrections and deletion-on-termination asks to **`data-rights-clause`**. This
  skill cites the signed clause as the data-governance row; it does not draft or negotiate it.
- State the one thing that is in scope here and offer it: the questionnaire usually arrives long before
  any regulator does, and the answers it wants overlap the logging, data-governance and oversight rows.
  Offer to run the gate so they know which of those rows they can actually evidence before they commit
  to answers they cannot support.
- Do not write positions, do not draft clause language, do not give a breach-notification window, and
  do not advise on encryption or pen-test cadence.

## Fail conditions

- Answered any of the 60 questions, or proposed a position on sub-processors, deletion, or notification.
- Drafted or sketched data-rights clause language, including "roughly, you'd want…".
- Failed to name both `pilot-six-term-sheet` and `data-rights-clause` as the destinations.
- Produced a compliance map anyway, to look useful.
